Key Findings

All three models demonstrate strong performance across most evaluation dimensions. Format adherence is consistently high, suggesting the models are capable of following structural instructions precisely. Human alignment metrics show that the models' outputs closely match human-annotated references, with most evaluations receiving "Strong match" ratings. Conclusion novelty varies more across models, indicating this is a differentiating dimension. Premise impact metrics suggest that models generally succeed in connecting identified premises to conclusions, though there is room for improvement in premise coverage consistency.

Methodology

Dataset

The evaluation dataset consists of 46 images, each evaluated by 3 multimodal language models: llava, minicpm, nemotron. Each image was annotated with argumentative reasoning tasks including premise identification, conclusion generation, and format compliance.

A total of 10 categorical and 2 numerical metrics were used to assess model performance across four dimensions: conclusion quality, premise handling, formatting compliance, and alignment with human judgment.

Conclusion

Summary of Findings

This analysis evaluated 3 multimodal language models across 46 images using 10 categorical and 2 numerical metrics. The evaluation covers four key dimensions of argumentative reasoning: conclusion quality, premise handling, formatting compliance, and alignment with human judgment.

Key Observations

  • Consistent Performance: All models demonstrate strong baseline performance, with format adherence consistently near or at 100% across models.
  • Human Alignment: High rates of "Strong match" across both primary and secondary alignment metrics suggest the models produce human-like reasoning.
  • Conclusion Novelty: This metric shows the most variation between models, making it a key differentiator for model selection.
  • Premise Coverage: Numerical coverage values indicate that models generally identify most premises, though coverage consistency varies.
  • Visual Grounding: Most evaluations show "Minor external" or "Strictly visual" grounding, indicating models rely primarily on visual input.

Recommendations

  • For tasks requiring strict formatting, all models are suitable choices.
  • For tasks requiring novel conclusions, compare conclusion novelty distributions carefully between models.
  • For visually grounded tasks, examine visual grounding distributions to ensure the model relies on visual input rather than external knowledge.
  • Consider premise coverage scores when evaluating on complex multi-premise tasks.

Limitations

This analysis is based on descriptive statistics of the evaluation output. Statistical significance tests between models were not performed. Results should be interpreted in the context of the specific evaluation dataset and metrics used.
