Paper Update • Section 6

Automated Evaluation of Visual Arguments

How we tested 3 Vision-Language Models against 2 Human Annotators across 46 climate images — without relying on BLEU/ROUGE.

The Big Picture

Why We Needed 4 Different Checks

Word-overlap metrics (BLEU/ROUGE) fail when many valid arguments exist for one image.

1. Logic Check (NLI)

Do the generated conclusions logically follow from the premises without contradicting them?

2. Scene Check (CLIPScore)

Are the premises and conclusions actually anchored to what is shown in the image?

3. Human Match (BERTScore / Cosine)

Do the models arrive at the same climate message as human annotators?

4. Hallucination Audit (CHAIR + POPE)

Do models invent fake objects or argue from things missing in the photo?

Setup: 46 text-free climate images • 2 Human Annotators • LLaVA-1.5 (7B), MiniCPM-V 2.6 (7.6B), Nemotron-3 Nano (30B)
6.1 Logic & Inference

Models Never Contradict — But Some Just Paraphrase

Tested with DeBERTa-v3 NLI between premises and conclusions
0.0%
Contradiction rate across all models & humans
  • Why "Neutral" dominates: Good climate conclusions infer beyond visible pixels, so NLI labels creative inferences as Neutral.
  • MiniCPM-V (13.7% entailment) matches Human 1 (15.9%) — making real interpretive leaps.
  • Nemotron (54.3% entailment) plays it safe by mostly restating its own premises.
Takeaway: NLI confirms zero self-contradiction, while distinguishing "creative inference" from "safe paraphrasing."

6.3 Human Alignment

A 7.6B Local Model Matches Human Meaning Best

Semantic similarity (MPNet Cosine & RoBERTa BERTScore) vs. human conclusions
0.906
MiniCPM-V 2.6 BERTScore F1
(Human-to-Human upper bound: 0.909)
  • Bigger ≠ Better: MiniCPM-V (7.6B) beats 30B Nemotron in matching human conclusions.
  • Format matters: Nemotron failed output formatting 48% of the time, producing fewer conclusions.
  • No single truth: Different models capture different valid angles of the same climate photo.
Takeaway: Well-structured local models can reach near-human semantic alignment on visual climate arguments.
6.4 New Addition • CHAIR + POPE Audit

Models Invent Absent Objects & Argue From "Nothing"

Verified every mentioned physical noun in the image using Grounding DINO
30–36%
Unverified objects in VLMs
(vs. ~20.5% human detector baseline)
  • Stereotype Hallucinations (Nemotron): Invents steam / heat waves or bottles & bags not in the image.
  • Absence Reasoning (LLaVA — 13.6%): Argues from missing items like no fishing equipment or no hurricanes (2× human rate).
  • MiniCPM-V is cleanest: Lowest hallucination rate (29.6%) and human-like restraint.
Why this matters: Proves statistically that VLMs rely on learned climate stereotypes instead of strictly visible evidence.
Summary Comparison

Three Models, Three Distinct Behaviors

What each model is good at — and where it breaks

MiniCPM-V 2.6 (7.6B)

Best Overall Balance

• Closest to human conclusions
• Lowest object hallucination
• Draws genuine inferences

Nemotron-3 Nano (30B)

Detailed but Over-Speculative

• Highest global CLIPScore
• Most object hallucinations (35.6%)
• Weak format adherence (52%)

LLaVA-1.5 (7B)

Relies on Negative Cues

• Follows format reliably
• Argues from absent objects (13.6%)
• Occasional object fabrications

Core Conclusion: No single automated metric is enough — they complement each other and justify our 10-metric human evaluation.
