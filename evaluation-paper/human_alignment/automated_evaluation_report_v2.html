Evaluation Methodology & Aggregation Protocol

To complement and validate our manual Human Alignment ratings, we introduced an automated semantic similarity evaluation comparing model-generated conclusions against human-written conclusions across all N = 46 images. Because both models and human annotators produce a variable number of conclusions per image (typically 1–3 claims), rigid one-to-one positional sentence matching is unsuitable.

Step 1 • Per Image
Pairwise Similarity Matrix
Compare every generated conclusion M against every human conclusion H to form matrix S ∈ ℝ|M|×|H|.
→
Step 2 • Image-Level
Two Aggregations
Extract Max (best single match) and Mean-Max (average best match across all model claims).
→
Step 3 • Corpus-Level
Dataset Summary (N=46)
Average image-level scores across all 46 images against Human 1, Human 2, and Combined references.

Max Similarity

Recall / Coverage
Max(S) = maxi,j Si,j

Following the multi-reference maximum-score protocol (Zhang et al., 2020; Fabbri et al., 2021), takes the single highest pairwise score per image. Indicates whether the model generates at least one conclusion aligned with a human reference.

Mean-Max Similarity

Greedy Precision
Mean-Max(S) = (1 / |M|) Σi=1..|M| maxj Si,j

Analogous to greedy precision matching (Zhang et al., 2020), identifies the best human reference match for each generated conclusion and averages them. Penalizes models that dilute strong claims with irrelevant conclusions.

Embedding Backbones & Baselines: Pairwise scores are computed using both Sentence-BERT Cosine Similarity (all-mpnet-base-v2; Reimers & Gurevych, 2019) and BERTScore F1 (RoBERTa-large; Zhang et al., 2020). Each model is evaluated against Human 1, Human 2, and a Combined reference pool (H1 ∪ H2). We also evaluate Human 1 vs. Human 2 to establish an empirical inter-annotator upper bound.

Key Takeaways for Discussion

01

Strong Agreement with Manual Human Alignment

MiniCPM-V 2.6 achieves the highest overall semantic alignment with human-authored conclusions, reaching a Cosine Max of 0.7325 and a BERTScore F1 of 0.9062 against the combined human references—nearly matching the inter-annotator human upper bound (0.9090). This independently corroborates our manual evaluation, where MiniCPM-V 2.6 also led on Human Alignment (91.3% and 93.5%).

02

Format Adherence Explains Nemotron's High Mean-Max Score

While Nemotron-3 Nano records the lowest BERTScore F1 (0.8908 combined), it achieves the highest Mean-Max Cosine similarity against Human 2 (0.6268) and the Combined pool (0.6587). Because Nemotron frequently violated output formatting rules (52.2% adherence) and generated fewer conclusions per image, its precision per generated claim stayed high, whereas MiniCPM-V 2.6 generated fuller multi-conclusion sets that covered more human interpretations (higher Max) while occasionally introducing broader secondary claims.

03

Interpretive Plurality in Environmental Imagery

Inter-annotator cosine similarity between Human 1 and Human 2 is 0.6644, whereas MiniCPM-V 2.6 reaches 0.7325 against the combined human pool. This demonstrates that environmental images support multiple valid argumentative angles rather than a single ground-truth conclusion, and strong VLMs successfully align with at least one human perspective.
