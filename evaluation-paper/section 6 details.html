6.1 Conclusion Validity via NLI

To complement manual evaluation of Conclusion Validity, we use a pre-trained Natural Language Inference (NLI) model—DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli (Laurer et al., 2024)—to test logical consistency between generated premises and conclusions. For each image and source (3 VLMs and 2 human annotators), all premises are concatenated as the NLI premise and evaluated against each generated conclusion to obtain softmax probabilities for Entailment, Neutral, and Contradiction.

6.1.1 Key Findings

  • Zero Contradictions: Both human annotators and all three VLMs exhibit a 0.0% predicted contradiction rate (mean contradiction probability < 0.005), confirming internal logical consistency.
  • Why "Neutral" Dominates: Because our task explicitly requires conclusions to infer beyond directly visible premises to articulate climate implications, valid novel inferences are typically labeled Neutral rather than deductive Entailment by standard NLI classifiers—making NLI effective for contradiction filtering, though still requiring human judgment for inductive validity.
  • Novelty vs. Paraphrasing Trade-off: MiniCPM-V 2.6 (13.7% entailment, mean 0.1388) and LLaVA-1.5 (17.0% entailment, mean 0.1725) closely mirror Human 1 (15.9%), indicating conclusions that introduce genuine interpretive leaps rather than restating premises (consistent with MiniCPM-V's high manual Conclusion Novelty). Conversely, Nemotron-3 Nano shows high entailment (54.3%), reflecting more conservative, closely paraphrased conclusions.

6.2 Visual Grounding via CLIPScore

To verify whether generated premises and conclusions stay anchored to visible image content, we compute reference-free CLIPScore (Hessel et al., 2021) using CLIP ViT-L-14 (Radford et al., 2021). We evaluate premise and conclusion similarity separately, track the minimum-scoring premise per image to catch isolated ungrounded claims, and compute the grounding shift (Δ = Prem. − Conc.).

6.2.1 Key Findings

  • Nemotron-3 Nano achieves the highest global image-text similarity (0.59 premises, 0.60 conclusions), though with higher variance across images (SD = 0.12).
  • MiniCPM-V 2.6 tracks human baselines most closely (0.57 premises, 0.56 conclusions) and is the only model with a slight positive grounding shift (Δ = +0.01), moving cleanly from concrete visual observations to abstract climate conclusions.
  • LLaVA-1.5 records the lowest minimum premise score (0.46) and largest negative shift (Δ = −0.05), signaling isolated premises that drift away from visible scene elements.

6.3 Human Alignment via Semantic Similarity

To quantify how closely model conclusions align with human interpretations, we compute sentence-level Cosine Similarity (all-mpnet-base-v2) and token-level BERTScore F1 (RoBERTa-large) between all candidate–reference conclusion pairs per image. Following multi-reference evaluation protocols (Zhang et al., 2020; Fabbri et al., 2021), we aggregate per image using Max (best match to any human conclusion) and Mean-Max (average best human match across all generated conclusions) before averaging across all N = 46 images.

6.4 Absent-Object Hallucination (CHAIR & POPE Audit)

During qualitative inspection, we observed that models occasionally assert non-existent visual elements or argue from the absence of objects at more than double the rate of human annotators—likely driven by top-down prior expectations rather than bottom-up visual evidence (Anderson et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2024). Because global CLIPScore can reward contextually plausible keywords even when specific objects are missing, we conducted a targeted two-stage verification audit combining the metric structure of CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) with the presence-probing paradigm of POPE (Li et al., 2023):

  1. Object & Negation Parsing: Using spaCy (en_core_web_lg), we extract concrete physical nouns mentioned in each premise (filtering abstract nouns) and flag explicit absence/negation cues (e.g., "no", "without", "absence of").
  2. Open-Vocabulary Visual Verification: Rather than restricting checks to closed-set COCO categories, we query Grounding DINO (grounding-dino-base) (Liu et al., 2024b) at confidence threshold τ = 0.30 to verify whether each extracted noun is visually localized in the image, computing the instance-level absent-object rate:
CHAIRi = |{hallucinated objects}| / |{all mentioned objects}| (Equation 1)

6.4.1 Key Findings & Qualitative Failure Examples

  • Consistent Hallucination Gap: Human annotations establish a baseline of 20.1%–20.9% detector-unverified nouns and 5.3%–6.3% negation-based premises. All three VLMs exceed human absent-object rates by +9.5 to +15.5 percentage points.
  • MiniCPM-V 2.6 is Most Grounded: Achieves the lowest model absent-object rate (29.6%) and stays closest to human negation frequency (8.2%).
  • Nemotron-3 Nano Over-Specifies Objects: Despite topping holistic CLIPScore, Nemotron mentions nearly twice as many physical nouns (734 across 109 premises) and incurs the highest absent-object rate (35.6%).
  • LLaVA-1.5 Relies Heavily on Absence Premises: Uses negation framing in 13.6% of premises—more than double the human rate.
