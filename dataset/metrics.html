Image-Argument Evaluation Metrics

8 Metrics with 4-Point Scales — Based on Argument Quality Literature

Output Format

Format Adherence

What it measures: Did the model follow the required output format?

LabelMeaning
PerfectOutput exactly matches all format requirements
Minor errorsMostly correct format with 1-2 small deviations
Partial failureFormat is partially correct but has notable violations
Complete failureOutput ignores format rules entirely
References
ArgGenBench (2026) — Instruction Following
Jin, B., et al. ACL 2026.
Instruction Following (IF)
"win/tie/lose rate against reference based on topic, stance, and each control signal (length, style, strategy, audience, key points)."

Our adaptation: Measures whether the model follows structural output rules independent of content quality.

Wachsmuth et al. (2017) — Clarity
EACL 2017.
Effectiveness → Clarity
"The argument is linguistically clear and as simple as possible without losing content."

Quantity Compliance

What it measures: Did the model generate the correct number of premises/conclusions?

LabelMeaning
All counts correctBoth counts fall within the specified range
~2 offAt least one count outside range, deviation is small
>2 offCounts deviate significantly from the range
Ignores limitsNo attempt to follow quantity rules
References
ArgGenBench (2026) — Length Fidelity
Jin, B., et al. ACL 2026.
Length Fidelity
"correct length adherence."

Our adaptation: Measures whether the model generates the correct NUMBER of premises and conclusions.

Wildfeuer & Coffie (2022) — Annotation Scheme
Frontiers in Communication.
Structural constraints
"annotation scheme for the description and annotation of both technical details..."
Premises

Premise Accuracy

What it measures: Are the described premises actually visible in the image?

LabelMeaning
All correctEvery premise describes something clearly visible
Mostly correctMost premises are visible; 1-2 minor errors
Partially correctSome visible, but several describe things not in image
Mostly wrongMost premises describe things not visible
References
Wachsmuth et al. (2017) — Local Acceptability
EACL 2017. ACL
Cogency → Local Acceptability
"The premises are worthy being believed as true."
"This does not mean that they must be true, but that the audience has no reason to reject them."

Our adaptation: Maps "premises worthy of being believed" to "premises describing things visible in the image."

Premise Completeness

What it measures: Did the model identify the main visible elements?

LabelMeaning
Captures allPremises cover all or almost all important visible elements
Captures mostCovers main elements with 1-2 notable omissions
Captures someCovers some but misses several important ones
Misses mostFails to capture the main visible content
References
Wildfeuer & Pollaroli (2017) — Argument Reconstruction
John Benjamins, pp. 189-216.
Reconstruction of logical units
"aims at a reconstruction of the logical units that guide the viewer's imagination"

Our adaptation: Measures whether model captured all important visual elements needed for the argument.

Wildfeuer (2018) — Rhetorical Structure
Semiotica 220, pp. 95-121.
Making premises available
"how premises and arguments for the interpretation are made available in multimodal context"
Conclusion

Conclusion Validity

What it measures: Does the conclusion logically follow from the premises?

LabelMeaning
Clearly followsDirect and logical inference from the premises
Mostly followsFollows with minor logical gaps or assumptions
Weakly followsBarely follows; requires significant assumptions
Does not followUnrelated to or contradicts the premises
References
Alshomary & Stahl (2022) — Validity
ArgMining 2022.
Task Definition: Validity
"Whether a given conclusion can be logically inferred from its premises (valid/invalid)."

Our adaptation: Uses 4-point scale instead of binary to capture degrees of logical connection.

Wachsmuth et al. (2017) — Local Sufficiency
EACL 2017.
Cogency → Local Sufficiency
"The premises are sufficient to draw the conclusion."
Gurcke et al. (2021) — Sufficiency Assessment
ArgMining 2021.
Human Evaluation Protocol
"Are the premises sufficient to draw the conclusion? Scale: 1-5"

Conclusion Novelty

What it measures: Does the conclusion add new insight beyond restating the premises?

LabelMeaning
InsightfulProvides genuinely new understanding or interpretation
ModerateAdds some interpretation beyond what premises state
ParaphraseMostly restates or rephrases the premises
CopyNearly identical to the premises
References
Alshomary & Stahl (2022) — Novelty
ArgMining 2022.
Task Definition: Novelty
"A valid but NOT novel conclusion = repetition, paraphrase, or summary of the premise."
"A valid AND novel conclusion = offers new information extending what is covered by the premise."
Gurcke et al. (2021) — Composition
ArgMining 2021.
Novelty/Composition
"Scale: 1 = verbatim copying, 2 = synonymous copying, 3 = copying + fusion, 4 = inference, 5 = cannot be composed"

Our adaptation: Collapses 5-point scale into 4 novelty levels.

Visual Grounding

What it measures: Does the conclusion stay grounded in the visual evidence?

LabelMeaning
Strictly visualConclusion follows directly from visible content
Minor externalMostly visual with small, reasonable inferences
Some externalMix of visual evidence and external knowledge
Heavy externalRelies mostly on external knowledge; barely connects to image
References
Tseronis et al. (2024) — Cognitive Dimension
Journal of Argumentation in Context.
Cognitive dimension
"how effectively the semiotic design helps the addressee process the argument."

Our adaptation: Measures whether conclusion stays grounded in visual evidence.

Wildfeuer & Pollaroli (2018) — Dynamic Context
International Review of Pragmatics 10(2).
Context-dependent interpretation
"multimodal arguments depend on visual context"

Human Alignment

What it measures: How closely does the model's reasoning match human interpretation?

LabelMeaning
Strong matchBoth reasoning and conclusion align with human interpretation
Conclusion onlySimilar conclusion but through different reasoning
Different focusFocuses on different aspects than a human would
UnrelatedNothing in common with human interpretation
References
Tseronis et al. (2024) — Cognitive Dimension
Journal of Argumentation in Context.
Cognitive dimension
"how effectively the semiotic design helps the addressee process the argument."
Lauscher et al. (2020) — Effectiveness
COLING 2020.
Annotation Protocol: Effectiveness
"Is the argument stated in a way that would be persuasive to the target audience?"

Our adaptation: Narrows "persuasive to target audience" to "similar to how a human would reason."

Wildfeuer (2018) — Inference Process
Semiotica 220, pp. 95-121.
Retracing inference process
"retraces the inference process of the recipient in detail"
